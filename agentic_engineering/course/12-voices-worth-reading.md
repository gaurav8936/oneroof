# Voices worth reading

## Why read the people behind the ideas

The tools change every month, but a handful of people explain the underlying ideas clearly and in public. Reading
them first-hand beats any summary, including this one. Here are twelve worth starting with.

## On working with models day to day

Simon Willison, a co-creator of the Django web framework, writes almost daily about using language models in practice
and gave prompt injection its name. Andrej Karpathy wrote the essay "Software 2.0" and coined the phrase "vibe coding".
Ethan Mollick, a professor at Wharton, wrote the book "Co-Intelligence" about working alongside AI.

## On building agent tools

Boris Cherny created Claude Code at Anthropic. Mitchell Hashimoto, a co-founder of HashiCorp, built the Ghostty
terminal, whose rendering library One Roof uses. Harrison Chase co-founded LangChain, and Jerry Liu co-founded
LlamaIndex, two early frameworks for connecting models to tools and data.

## On reliability and memory

Hamel Husain writes and teaches about evaluations: testing AI products the way you test software. Jason Liu created
Instructor, a library for getting structured, validated output from models. Charles Packer led the MemGPT research on
long-term memory for agents and co-founded Letta.

## On the bigger picture

Shawn Wang, known as swyx, wrote "The Rise of the AI Engineer" and co-hosts the Latent Space podcast. Andrew Ng has
taught widely on agentic workflows such as reflection, tool use and planning. Dario Amodei's essay "Machines of Loving
Grace" lays out why all of this might matter. Anthropic's article "Building effective agents" is a practical place to
start.
